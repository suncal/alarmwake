Setting Fajr in Marseille

Fajr is the reason most people land on a page like this. Press Alarm on the Fajr row and it becomes a real alarm in this tab, with the sound and volume you choose. Tomorrow's Fajr shifts by a minute or two, so set it the night before rather than leaving one alarm running all week, or use the habit alarm to keep a streak going.

Marseille is at latitude 43.30, and the length of twilight there is what sets Fajr apart from sunrise. The table shows sunrise as well, because that is the end of the Fajr window.

How the times are worked out

The times come from the position of the sun for your latitude and longitude on today's date, computed in your browser. Nothing is sent anywhere and nothing is fetched, so the page keeps working with no connection once it has loaded.

Fajr and Isha depend on how far the sun sits below the horizon at dawn and dusk, and authorities differ on the angle, which is why the calculation method matters. Dhuhr follows the sun crossing its highest point, Maghrib follows sunset, and Asr depends on the length of an object's shadow: the standard opinion uses one shadow length, the Hanafi opinion two, so the two can be nearly an hour apart in winter.

These are calculated times, not a ruling. Where your local mosque publishes a timetable, follow it: committees often round to the safe side or apply a fixed adjustment for the horizon. Use this to set the alarm, and the timetable to settle the minute.

At high latitudes in summer, twilight can last all night and there is no true Fajr or Isha angle. The table shows a dash rather than inventing a time; follow the convention your community uses.

Making the Fajr alarm actually wake you

Setting a time is the easy part. Put the device across the room, turn the volume up, and press Test sound once at the volume you will be sleeping at, because a silent phone is the usual reason an alarm is missed. Choose a loud sound if you sleep heavily, or turn on the heavy-sleeper mode, which adds a siren, a screen that brightens like a sunrise beforehand, and sums you have to solve before it stops.

A web page cannot ring once the browser is closed, so leave this tab open, or turn on the backup notification in Settings, which our server sends at the alarm time even if the tab is gone. The sleep calculator works out when to go to bed for a given wake time in whole sleep cycles.

Questions

Will the alarm ring if I close the tab or the laptop?

No. No website can ring after the browser is closed or the computer sleeps. Keep this tab open, plug in, and the clock keeps the screen awake for you. The Ready-to-ring check under the clock shows what is set up correctly.

Does it work on my phone?

Yes on Android while the tab is open (behind other tabs is fine). On iPhone the page must stay in the foreground because Safari does not let web pages play sound from the background. Add it to the Home Screen for a full-screen bedside clock.

Is it loud enough for heavy sleepers?

Pick Loud buzzer or Siren, set the volume slider to full, and switch on the sums-to-stop option in Settings so the alarm cannot be dismissed half asleep. The sunrise light brightens the screen before the alarm too.

Can I use my own sound?

Yes. In Settings, choose an MP3, WAV or OGG file. It is stored in this browser only and never uploaded. There are also 60 built-in sounds, from a twin-bell alarm clock and a rooster to an air horn and a music box, on the sounds page.

What do the sleep sounds do?

Rain, brown noise, white noise, fan and ocean are generated in the browser and play until the alarm, then fade out as it rings. They keep the tab active through the night.

Does it need an internet connection?

Only to load the page the first time. Alarms, sounds, the sleep calculator and the sunrise math all run on your device, and the page works offline once it has been opened.

Is anything stored or sent?

Alarms and settings are saved in your browser's local storage. Nothing is sent to a server, and there is no account.

What time is Fajr in Marseille today?

The table above shows it for today, calculated for Marseille's coordinates using Muslim World League. It moves by a minute or two each day, so it is worth checking rather than reusing yesterday's.

Are these times official for Marseille?

They are calculated from the sun's position, which is how published timetables are produced, but committees often round or apply a small adjustment. Where your mosque publishes a timetable, follow that and use this page to set the alarm.

Can I set all five at once?

Yes. The hub page has a button that creates all five as separate alarms, each labelled with its prayer.
